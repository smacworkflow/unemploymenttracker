The thesis this dashboard tests
Unemployment rises not from mass layoffs but from a hiring freeze / no-backfill world: firms don't replace quits, firings, or retirements. The fingerprint is a labor market where the level of unemployment grinds up while every "firing" gauge stays benign and every "finding/hiring" gauge deteriorates. Formally, with u* ≈ s/(s+f), the separation rate s stays low while the job-finding rate f collapses.
Confirmed when: initial claims & layoff rate stay low, while hires rate, quits rate and total churn fall and unemployment duration lengthens. Falsified (ordinary recession) when: claims and separation/layoff rates spike. Watch for the head-fake: if prime-age employment-to-population falls, discouraged workers exit and U-3 understates the damage — anchor on EPOP and duration.

1 · Hires rate — the finding-side proxy

Falling hires with stable layoffs = the freeze. No clean single "job-finding rate" on FRED; hires rate is the cleanest available stand-in.

2 · Hires vs. layoffs & discharges

Structural tell: hires fall while layoffs stay flat-low. In a normal recession both rise together.

3 · Quits rate — churn / confidence

People stop quitting when there's nowhere to go. Low quits alongside rising unemployment is a freeze signal, not a hot-market one.

4 · Total churn (hires + separations)

The single best summary of a "sclerotic" labor market: low hiring and low separations grinding down together.

5 · Duration: 27+ week share & median weeks

The mechanical consequence of a low finding rate — the unemployed get stuck longer even before the level moves much.

6 · Vacancy-to-unemployment (V/U)

Openings per unemployed person. A collapsing ratio — especially a Beveridge-curve shift — signals matching deterioration.

7 · U-6 minus U-3 gap

As discouraged/part-time slack builds, the gap widens and the headline rate hides damage.

8 · Prime-age (25–54) EPOP & participation

The head-fake guard. If these fall, dropouts are suppressing U-3 — anchor here instead of the headline.

9 · Continuing ÷ initial jobless claims

Flat initial claims (nobody firing) but a rising ratio = the newly unemployed can't get rehired. High-frequency; history builds forward from this snapshot.
